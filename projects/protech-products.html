Engineering problem

Challenge & design intent

Problem

Service equipment had to be safe, functional, manufacturable, serviceable, and compatible with available materials and shop processes.

Design intent

Create production-oriented mechanical designs that could move cleanly from CAD into fabrication, assembly, testing, and technician use.

Constraints

  • Available materials and fabrication capability
  • Hydraulic/pneumatic routing
  • Fit-up and assembly clearances
  • Component sourcing
  • Cost and lead time
  • Service use and safety
Role

What I owned

  • Designed hydraulic and linear-actuator motorcycle service ramps and related shop equipment.
  • Prepared parts, assemblies, fabrication drawings, layouts, and BOMs in SolidWorks.
  • Worked with sheet-metal punching/bending, CNC operations, machining, powder coating, and assembly.
  • Adjusted geometry, clearances, and mounting features based on prototype fit-up and technician feedback.
  • Supported component sourcing and supply-chain coordination.
Why this project matters

This project demonstrates my preferred engineering workflow: connect system requirements to mechanical design, move the design into real hardware, observe actual behavior, and use evidence from fabrication or testing to guide the next iteration.

Outcome

Result

Developed manufacturable equipment by continuously connecting CAD decisions to fabrication capability, assembly feedback, service use, availability, lead time, and cost.
