Engineering problem

Challenge & design intent

Problem

Low-power ambient sources could not directly support the short, higher-power burst required for a reliable CAN status message during an outage or trigger event.

Design intent

Create a modular backup architecture that harvests thermal, vibration, and solar energy, conditions each source, stores energy, and activates the communication load only above a safe threshold.

Constraints

  • Very low harvested power
  • Different voltage/power behavior across three sources
  • Brownout risk
  • Small energy budget
  • Need for threshold-based activation
Role

What I owned

  • Defined the three-source architecture using TEG, piezoelectric, and photovoltaic inputs.
  • Integrated source-specific power conditioning paths.
  • Selected and evaluated supercapacitor storage and ATtiny MCU + CAN load behavior.
  • Used threshold/UVLO logic so the load operated only when sufficient energy was stored.
  • Built the test setup, captured source behavior, and evaluated charge-discharge-recovery cycles.

Outcome

Result

Testing confirmed repeatable charge-discharge-recovery behavior. Solar dominated charging when available, the TEG provided persistent baseline harvesting, and reliable CAN operation depended on stored energy remaining above the activation threshold.
