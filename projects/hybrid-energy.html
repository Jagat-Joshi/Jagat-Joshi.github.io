Hybrid Energy Harvesting Backup Power System

Maintain CAN communication during a power outage using harvested energy from multiple low-power sources.

The Problem

Critical low-power communication hardware can lose functionality during outages, while ambient energy sources are individually intermittent and low-output.

Design Intent

Create a hybrid architecture combining thermoelectric, solar, and vibration harvesting with energy storage and regulated power delivery.

What I Did

Integrated source characterization, power-management electronics, storage behavior, microcontroller loads, and CAN communication into a working experimental setup. Built test procedures, collected charge/discharge data, and troubleshot startup, wiring, transient-load, and repeatability issues.

Key Results

Demonstrated hybrid storage charging from approximately 1.0 V to 3.2 V and evaluated the energy required for CAN events. The work established a practical backup-power architecture rather than relying on a single harvesting source.

Tools

MATLAB/Simulink, LabVIEW, CAN, power electronics, instrumentation, experimental testing
